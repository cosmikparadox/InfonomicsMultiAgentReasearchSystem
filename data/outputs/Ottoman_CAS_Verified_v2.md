## DOMAIN 1: GOVERNANCE/POLITICAL

### Rise Phase (1299–1520) — CAS Score: 4.0/5 [Confidence: HIGH]

**Core Findings (Verified)**:
- **Devşirme system**: Recruited 200,000–300,000 Christian boys over ~300 years (c.1380–1683). Converted to Islam, trained in Enderun Palace School or Janissary corps. Created a slave-elite loyal to the sultan rather than hereditary aristocracy. [Confidence: MODERATE — aggregate numbers are estimates; İnalcık and Vatin/Veinstein are primary authorities]
- **Timar system**: Formalized 1362–1413. Land grants in exchange for military service; revocable, non-hereditary. By 1528, timar revenues supported 90,000+ cavalry. State revenue ~9.65M Venetian ducats (Barkan 1953-54). [Confidence: HIGH — budget data survives]
- **Administrative meritocracy**: Grand Viziers from devşirme backgrounds; rotation of provincial governors; kadi system for judicial oversight. Fratricide (later kafes) practice controlled succession crises and elite overproduction (Turchin & Nefedov 2009). [Confidence: HIGH]

**Counter-Evidence**:
1. **Devşirme was coercive, not meritocratic** — Forced separation, mandatory conversion, absence of consent. Served sultanic control rather than genuine merit (Yilmaz, Georgetown repository). Varied outcomes: some became Grand Vizier, others ended as laborers. [Strength: STRONG; Confidence: HIGH]
2. **Tezcan's "Second Ottoman Empire"** — The post-1580 transformation was proto-democratization, not decline. Janissaries, ulema, and merchants gained political participation. Deposition of Osman II (1622) = constitutional awareness, not decay. [Strength: STRONG; Confidence: HIGH]
3. **Salzmann's alternative modernization** — Provincial ayan rise was not governance failure but alternative path to modernity; "rival paths to the modern state." [Strength: STRONG; Confidence: HIGH]

### Peak Phase (1520–1683) — CAS Score: 4.5/5 [Confidence: HIGH]

**Core Findings (Verified)**:
- Süleyman I's reign (1520–1566): "Classical Age" of institutional maturity
- Dual legal system: Kanun (secular) + Sharia codified into comprehensive framework
- Administrative hierarchy: 4 levels (Sultan/Divan → Beylerbey → Sanjak-bey → Kaza/Kadi); ~32 eyalets
- Tax collection efficiency: 46% of assessed reaching central treasury (1527/28) — HIGH confidence (Karaman & Pamuk 2010)
- Post-1580 deterioration: Great Debasement, Celali rebellions, sale of offices beginning

### Decline Phase (1683–1922) — CAS Score: 2.5/5 [Confidence: HIGH]

**Core Findings (Verified)**:
- Tax collection: Only 25% reaching central treasury by 1661/62 (Karaman & Pamuk 2010) — HIGH confidence
- Malikane system (1695): Lifetime tax farms created hereditary fiscal extractors
- Ayan power accumulation; five sultans deposed by ~1710
- Tanzimat reforms (1839–1876): Partial fiscal recentralization to ~35-40% collection
- Corruption/institutional decay: Scored 4.0/5 on decay scale (HIGH confidence)

## DOMAIN 2: LEGAL/INSTITUTIONAL

### Rise Phase (1299–1520) — CAS Score: 4.0/5 [Confidence: HIGH]

**Core Findings**:
- Dual Kanun + Sharia legal system providing comprehensive coverage
- Kanun codification under Mehmed II and Bayezid II
- Millet framework provided religious community self-governance for non-Muslims

**Counter-Evidence**:
1. **Kuran's "Long Divergence"** — Islamic inheritance law (fragmenting estates), partnership rules (no corporate personhood), rigid waqf provisions created structural barriers to economic modernization. Originally effective but became liability. [Strength: STRONG; Confidence: MODERATE]
2. **Millet "system" is retroactive invention** (Braude 1982) — Before 19th century, millet organization was "not at all systematic." The coherent system was a later political innovation. [Strength: STRONG; Confidence: HIGH]
3. **Legal pluralism as segregation** — Dhimmi status = systematic inequality, not tolerance. Millet structure contributed to nationalist movements that destroyed the empire. [Strength: MODERATE; Confidence: MODERATE]

### Peak Phase (1520–1683) — CAS Score: 4.0/5 [Confidence: HIGH]
- Süleyman "the Lawgiver" — comprehensive kanun codification
- Ebussuud Efendi reconciled Kanun and Sharia
- Cash waqf legitimization debate (1545-1548): institutional flexibility demonstrated
- Capitulations regime initiated 1536 — early sovereignty erosion

### Decline Phase (1683–1922) — CAS Score: 2.0/5 [Confidence: HIGH]
- Capitulations expanded; juridical sovereignty eroded
- 1838 Balta Liman: commercial sovereignty effectively lost
- 1881 OPDA: fiscal sovereignty lost (41 years before political dissolution)
- Tanzimat legal reforms: Mecelle code (1869-1876) = major modernization attempt

## DOMAIN 3: MILITARY/DEFENSE

### Rise Phase (1299–1520) — CAS Score: 4.5/5 [Confidence: HIGH]

**Core Findings (Verified)**:
- Janissaries: "Perhaps the first standing infantry equipped with firearms" (Ágoston 2005)
- Artillery by Bayezid I (1389-1402); Constantinople siege consumed 55,000 lbs gunpowder
- Tophane-i Amire: "Probably the largest military-industrial complex in the world" (Ágoston)
- Cross-cultural technology recruitment (Master Orban, European foundry masters)

**Counter-Evidence**:
1. **Military transformation not decay** (Aksan 2007) — Post-classical changes were adaptive responses, not deterioration. Comparative questions "not asked because of the assumption Ottoman reforms failed massively." [Strength: STRONG; Confidence: HIGH]
2. **Janissaries as socio-economic agents** (Spyropoulos/Kafadar) — By 18th century, Janissaries owned 17% of Istanbul shops. Economically dynamic urban agents, not purely military. [Strength: STRONG; Confidence: HIGH]
3. **Technology parity persisted** (Ágoston) — Ottoman military-industrial self-sufficiency continued into 18th century. "Third-tier producer" thesis (Krause) is wrong. [Strength: STRONG; Confidence: HIGH]

### Peak Phase (1520–1683) — CAS Score: 4.0/5 [Confidence: HIGH]
- Peak timar cavalry: 90,000+ [Confidence: MODERATE — estimates vary]
- Gunpowder self-sufficiency: Baghdad baruthane 270 metric tons/year by 1566
- Naval: 110 yards/arsenals; post-Lepanto fleet rebuilt in ~6 months
- Defence spending: 52-61% of central budget (1527); rising to 77% by 1630 (wartime)

### Decline Phase (1683–1922) — CAS Score: 2.5/5 [Confidence: HIGH]
- Post-Vienna reversal (1683); Karlowitz Treaty (1699) — first territorial losses
- Defence spending: 75% of state expenditure by 1784-85 (fiscal unsustainability)
- Nizam-i Cedid experiment (26,000 troops) destroyed 1807
- Mahmud II abolished Janissaries 1826 ("Auspicious Incident")
- Individual Janissary pay collapsed: 34.38 → 11.05 gold pieces (1582-1669)

## DOMAIN 4: ECONOMIC/FISCAL

### Rise Phase (1299–1520) — CAS Score: 3.5/5 [Confidence: MODERATE]

**Core Findings**:
- GDP per capita: ~$600 (1990 Int$) c.1500 — comparable to Southern Europe (Maddison/Pamuk)
- Akçe silver content stable at 1.15-1.20g (1326-1444); minor debasement under Mehmed II
- Government debt: ~0% of GDP (no external borrowing; timar + debasement seigniorage)
- Banking development: 1.0/5 (sarraf money-changers only)
- Capital market depth: 0.5/5 (no tradeable securities)

**Counter-Evidence**:
1. **Genç's "rational policy" thesis** — Ottoman economic principles (provisionism, fiscalism, traditionalism) were not "backward" but rational anti-capitalist policy choices. The economy functioned as designed. [Strength: STRONG; Confidence: HIGH]
2. **Sajdi's critique** — The entire "decline" framework is Eurocentric, measuring Ottoman economy against a European yardstick it never aspired to. [Strength: STRONG; Confidence: MODERATE]
3. **Salzmann/Darling** — Tax farming contained genuine market logic; it was not merely corruption. [Strength: MODERATE; Confidence: HIGH]

### Peak Phase (1520–1683) — CAS Score: 3.5/5 [Confidence: MODERATE]

**Key Quantitative Data (Verified)**:
- State revenue: 9.65M Venetian ducats (1528) — HIGH confidence
- Great Debasement 1585-86: akçe lost 44% silver content in single episode — HIGH confidence
- Cash waqf rates: 10-15% (istiglal); Muslim elite paradoxically charged MORE at 19-20% (Kuran & Rubin 2018)
- GDP growth: 0.00-0.10% annual — LOW confidence
- Govt revenue: 3-5% of GDP (central treasury cash only) — MODERATE confidence
- Trade balance: roughly balanced; capitulations at 3% duties — LOW confidence

### Decline Phase (1683–1922) — CAS Score: 2.0/5 [Confidence: HIGH]

**Key Quantitative Data (Verified)**:
- Currency debasement: akçe from 0.13g (1700) to 0.008g (1840s) = 94% loss — HIGH confidence
- First foreign loan: 1854, £3M at 6% (effective 7.5%) — HIGH confidence
- 1874 credit collapse: Galata bankers refused to lend even at 25% — HIGH confidence
- 1875 default: £200-214.5M nominal; debt service £12M = 50%+ of revenue — MODERATE confidence
- 1881 OPDA: Debt restructured from £252.8M to £106.4M; OPDA controlled 32-40% of revenues — HIGH confidence
- Post-OPDA rates: 4-6% (risk premium compressed from 5.7% to 2.1%) — MODERATE confidence
- Trade deficit post-1838: Imports grew ~8x vs exports ~4x after Balta Liman — MODERATE confidence
- GDP per capita 1913: $979-1,100 (Britain ~$5,000 by then) — MODERATE confidence

## DOMAIN 5: SOCIAL/CLASS

### Rise Phase (1299–1520) — CAS Score: 3.5/5 [Confidence: MODERATE]

- Askeri (military/administrative) vs reaya (tax-paying subjects) fundamental distinction
- Devşirme as social mobility mechanism for Christian subjects
- Guild (esnaf) organization provided urban social structure

**Counter-Evidence**:
1. **Faroqhi: social mobility persisted** — Multiple channels beyond devşirme: trade, religious learning, guild advancement. Social structure more fluid than askeri-reaya binary suggests. [Strength: MODERATE; Confidence: HIGH]
2. **Singer: massive waqf welfare** — 50-66% of all property held as waqf by some estimates; provided hospitals, soup kitchens, education. Extensive social safety net. [Strength: MODERATE; Confidence: MODERATE]

### Peak Phase (1520–1683) — CAS Score: 3.5/5 [Confidence: MODERATE]
- Social mobility at peak; Sultanate of Women period
- Coffee house culture from 1555 as social mixing institution

### Decline Phase (1683–1922) — CAS Score: 2.5/5 [Confidence: MODERATE]
- Askeri-reaya rigidification challenged by Tezcan (transformation not decay)
- Ayan class rise; provincial power structures
- Devşirme system effectively ended by late 17th century

## DOMAIN 7: CULTURAL/IDEOLOGICAL (GAP-FILLED)

### Rise Phase (1299–1520) — CAS Score: 3.5/5 [Confidence: HIGH]

**Core Findings** (New — previously thin):
- **Triple-heritage identity**: Islamic ghazi ideology + Turkic steppe traditions + Byzantine administrative inheritance (Kafadar 1995, Lowry 2003, Vryonis 1971)
- **Ghazi thesis debate**: Wittek (1938) vs Kafadar (1995) vs Lowry (2003) — fluid social ideology not monolithic religious category
- **Architectural patronage**: Green Mosque (Bursa, 1412-1419); Timurid artistic migration; külliye model
- **Mehmed II as Kayser-i Rûm**: claimed Roman imperial succession after 1453

**Counter-Evidence**:
- Kafadar warns against treating early Ottoman identity as fixed; it was "between two worlds"
- Lindner sees early state as primarily a predatory confederacy motivated by booty, not religion
- Byzantine cultural influence vs mere administrative borrowing is debated

### Peak Phase (1520–1683) — CAS Score: 4.5/5 [Confidence: HIGH]

**Core Findings** (New):
- **Ehl-i Hiref**: 40 artisan societies with 600+ members (1526 payroll records survive)
- **Mimar Sinan**: 300+ major structures; Kırkçeşme water system (55 km channels, 33 aqueducts); Selimiye designed to surpass Christian architecture (Necipoğlu 2005)
- **Süleymanname**: 65 gold-decorated miniature scenes; Ottoman dynastic history modeled on Iranian Shahnameh
- **Coffeehouses from 1555**: Proto-public sphere; *Mekteb-i irfan* ("school of knowledge"); state surveilled them via spies
- **Multi-layered legitimacy**: Islamic caliphal (post-1517) + Turkic dynastic + Roman imperial succession

**Counter-Evidence**:
- Persian remained language of high culture alongside Ottoman Turkish (Kafadar 2007)
- Coffeehouses were sites of political opposition; authorities periodically tried to close them
- Zilfi (2004): sumptuary laws reveal anxiety about social mixing

### Decline Phase (1683–1922) — CAS Score: 3.5/5 [Confidence: HIGH]

**Core Findings** (New):
- **Tulip Period (1718-1730)**: Yirmisekiz Çelebi Mehmed's Paris embassy; rococo architectural influence
- **Tanzimat identity crisis**: Gülhane Edict (1839) created structural duality; reforms promoted Ottomanism but accelerated separatism
- **Akçura's "Üç Tarz-i Siyaset" (1904)**: Ottomanism vs Pan-Islamism vs Pan-Turkism — unresolvable
- **Abdülhamid II Pan-Islamism**: Sophisticated legitimation strategy (Deringil) but more effective internationally than domestically

**Counter-Evidence**:
- Erimtan challenges Tulip Period as "wishful invention" of 20th-century intellectuals
- Young Turks used all three ideologies pragmatically and simultaneously
- 18th-century Ottoman culture was adaptive, not degenerating (revisionist view)

## DOMAIN 8: TECHNOLOGICAL/INNOVATION (GAP-FILLED)

### Rise Phase (1299–1520) — CAS Score: 4.0/5 [Confidence: HIGH]

**Core Findings** (New):
- Artillery by Bayezid I (1389-1402); siege of Constantinople: 55,000 lbs gunpowder, 5,000+ stones
- **Tophane-i Amire** (Royal Cannon Foundry): "probably the largest military-industrial complex in the world" (Ágoston 2005)
- Janissaries: first standing infantry equipped with firearms
- Deliberate cross-cultural technology recruitment (Orban, Jörg of Nuremberg, George of Frankfurt)

**Counter-Evidence**:
- Reliance on European foundry masters raises questions about indigenous vs adopted innovation
- Orban first offered services to Byzantines (Ottoman advantage was financial, not technical)
- Early innovations primarily military; civilian technology transfer limited

### Peak Phase (1520–1683) — CAS Score: 3.5/5 [Confidence: HIGH]

**Core Findings** (New):
- Gunpowder self-sufficiency: Baghdad baruthane 270 metric tons/year by 1566
- **Taqi al-Din observatory (1577-1580)**: Matched Tycho Brahe's instruments; first observational clock with seconds; first decimal point notation. **Demolished January 1580** — paradigmatic institutional failure to protect innovation
- Naval: 110 yards/arsenals; post-Lepanto fleet rebuilt in ~6 months
- Galley-dependence in Mediterranean: appropriate for context but path-dependent

**Counter-Evidence**:
- Observatory destruction: strongest counter-evidence to technological openness
- Technology gap did not emerge until c. 1680 (Ágoston, Murphey)
- Printing press not adopted for 275+ years (1450s Europe vs 1729 Ottoman)

### Decline Phase (1683–1922) — CAS Score: 2.5/5 [Confidence: HIGH]

**Core Findings** (New):
- **Printing press (1729)**: İbrahim Müteferrika; only 12,700 books across 17 works; restricted to non-religious topics
- **Nizam-i Cedid (1789-1807)**: 26,000 European-style troops; French instructors; "a profound shift in Ottoman thinking" (Shaw). **Destroyed by Janissary revolt 1807**
- Technology gap consensus: c. 1680 (Ágoston, Murphey), not earlier
- **Selective modernization**: Military first, telegraph rapidly (1855), railways via concessions, printing press very late
- Printing press delay explanations: Arabic script costs (Osborn 2022), calligraphy resistance (Juma), functional existing script system (Schwartz 2017)

**Counter-Evidence**:
- Murphey: defeats were primarily logistical, not technological
- Gunpowder self-sufficiency re-established in late 18th century (not irreversible)
- Nizam-i Cedid destruction was political, not cognitive — barrier was internal opposition
- Ágoston: "third-tier producer" dependency thesis is wrong

## DOMAIN 9: INFORMATION/COMMUNICATION (GAP-FILLED — Previously 1/5)

### Rise Phase (1299–1520) — CAS Score: 3.0/5 [Confidence: HIGH]

**Core Findings** (New — previously absent):
- **Tahrir defter system**: One of the most sophisticated pre-modern information-gathering systems. Three simultaneous register types: *mufassal* (detailed), *icmal* (summary), *vakıf* (endowment). Commissioners personally met all male adults. Copied and preserved at Defter-i Hakani (state archives). Conducted after conquests and every 20-30 years. (İnalcık, Barkan)
- "Few historical records are as rich, extensive, well-preserved, and widely available" (Coşgel)
- Early communication relied on mounted Crimean Tatar messengers

**Counter-Evidence**:
- Tahrir measured fiscal capacity, not economic output; designed for extraction not analysis
- Coverage uneven across territory
- Better at gathering information than using it for adaptive policy

### Peak Phase (1520–1683) — CAS Score: 3.5/5 [Confidence: HIGH]

**Core Findings** (New):
- **Menzilhane postal system**: Stations every 35 km; Tatar messengers under Tatar Ağa; formalized under Lütfi Pasha during Süleyman I's reign (Heywood)
- **Divan record-keeping**: Beylikçi kalemi drafted all imperial decrees; mühimme registers recorded all political/military/social/economic matters
- **Espionage networks**: Ottoman counter-intelligence with cryptography and steganography (Gürkan 2012); merchants, corsairs, travelers gathered intelligence
- **Venetian bailo intelligence**: Simultaneous diplomatic representation and intelligence operations; Ottoman state was both information gatherer and information target
- Land surveys expanded empire-wide in 1535 (100 years without interruption)

**Counter-Evidence**:
- Menzilhane served state, not public; private communication minimal
- Intelligence was decentralized and personality-dependent, not institutionalized
- Venetian penetration suggests significant counter-intelligence gaps

### Decline Phase (1683–1922) — CAS Score: 2.5/5 [Confidence: HIGH]

**Core Findings** (New):
- **Tahrir collapse post-1715**: Regular land surveys stopped; cadastral survey resumed 1858-1860
- **Census modernization**: 1830 first approaching "true census"; 1881-1893 first official modern census took 10 years to complete; identity cards (nüfus cüzdanları) enforced (Karpat)
- **Telegraph (1855)**: Adopted rapidly during Crimean War; by 1880s: ~20,000 miles wire, 3+ million messages/year; publicly owned (unlike most Ottoman infrastructure). "Development of territorial sovereignty" (Bektaş 2000)
- **Newspapers**: Takvim-i Vekayi (1831, Ottoman/French/Armenian/Greek); first private papers 1860
- **Hub-and-spoke pathology**: Center-periphery communication only; limited inter-provincial flows; 30% of tax revenues reaching government by 18th century
- Bureaucracy grew: 183 scribes (1593) → 1,500-2,000 (1790s) — more information processed even as control weakened

**Counter-Evidence**:
- Telegraph adopted within years of European deployment — "technology gap" must be domain-specific
- Revisionist historians argue 18th-century decentralization was different governance mode, not collapse (Yaycioglu)
- Newspaper press developed rapidly after 1860
