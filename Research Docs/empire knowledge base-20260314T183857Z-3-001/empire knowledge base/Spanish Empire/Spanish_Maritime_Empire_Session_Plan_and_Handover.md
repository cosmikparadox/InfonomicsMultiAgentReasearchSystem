## SPANISH MARITIME EMPIRE: 4-SESSION STRUCTURE

### Session 1: Foundational Tier (~8,000-10,000 words)
**Domains:** Governance, Legal, Military
**Cross-Cutting:** Energy, Legitimacy/Narrative

**Key Research Angles:**
1. **Governance (Revisionist Lens)**
   - Polysynodial council system effectiveness vs. dysfunction narrative
   - Habsburg composite monarchy as adaptive or maladaptive
   - Storrs' argument for administrative resilience
   - Philip II's "paper king" management style
   - Favorito system (Lerma, Olivares) — reform attempts or extraction?

2. **Legal/Institutional**
   - Laws of the Indies — extractive or governance innovation?
   - Encomienda/repartimiento evolution
   - Casa de Contratación as knowledge management institution
   - Property rights for peninsular vs. colonial populations
   - Audiencia system effectiveness

3. **Military**
   - Tercio as world-leading innovation (1503-1643)
   - Armada failure (1588) — strategic miscalculation or bad luck?
   - Army of Flanders sustainability costs
   - Naval power projection vs. Portuguese comparison
   - Military-fiscal integration (Thompson's framework)

4. **Energy** (Cross-Cutting)
   - Atlantic wind systems exploitation
   - Colonial extraction energy base (human labor, animal power)
   - Mining energy infrastructure (Potosí, Zacatecas)
   - Comparison to Portuguese wind-based maritime power

5. **Legitimacy/Narrative** (Cross-Cutting)
   - Catholic universalism as hegemonic ideology
   - Papal authority and Tordesillas legitimacy
   - Counter-Reformation as identity project
   - Crisis point: Treaty of Westphalia (1648) and narrative collapse

**Key Scholars to Engage:**
- **Revisionist:** Christopher Storrs, I.A.A. Thompson, Henry Kamen, Ruth MacKay
- **Traditional:** John Elliott, Geoffrey Parker
- **Military:** Parker's "Military Revolution" thesis, Thompson's "fiscal-military state"

### Session 2: Capability Tier (~8,000-10,000 words)
**Domains:** Social, Educational, Cultural, Technological, Demographic
**Cross-Cutting:** Information Infrastructure, External Shocks

**Key Research Angles:**
1. **Social/Class Structure**
   - Hidalgo mentality — how pervasive vs. regional?
   - Limpieza de sangre — actual enforcement vs. rhetorical?
   - Colonial caste system (peninsulares, criollos, mestizos)
   - Mobility pathways: Church, military, Indies trade

2. **Educational**
   - Salamanca, Alcalá — intellectual output and innovation
   - Jesuit educational network — global knowledge transfer
   - Colonial universities (Santo Domingo 1538, Lima 1551, Mexico 1551)
   - Scientific capacity vs. Inquisition constraints

3. **Cultural/Ideological**
   - Counter-Reformation cultural production
   - "Golden Age" literature and arts (Cervantes, Velázquez)
   - Attitudes toward commerce — regional variation
   - Religious uniformity vs. commercial pragmatism tension

4. **Technological**
   - Mining technology innovation (mercury amalgamation)
   - Manila galleon navigation
   - Shipbuilding capacity — Basque/Cantabrian yards
   - Technology transfer vs. independent development

5. **Demographic**
   - Population trajectory (pre-plague, post-plague, recovery)
   - Expulsion impacts (Jews 1492, Moriscos 1609-1614)
   - Colonial demographic disaster and recovery
   - Emigration to Americas — brain drain or expansion?

6. **Information Infrastructure** (Cross-Cutting)
   - Printing under Inquisition control — actual suppression levels
   - Casa de Contratación as geographic intelligence hub
   - Factor correspondence networks
   - News/gazette circulation (comparison to Dutch)

7. **External Shocks** (Cross-Cutting)
   - Plague cycles and institutional response
   - 1557 bankruptcy — first of many, institutional learning?
   - Armada 1588 — recovery capacity
   - Thirty Years' War strain

**Key Scholars to Engage:**
- Kamen on social history
- MacKay on commerce attitudes
- Parker on demographic/military
- Flynn & Giráldez on silver flows

### Session 3: Culmination Tier (~8,000-10,000 words)
**Domains:** Information/Communication, Economic/Fiscal/Financial
**Cross-Cutting:** Inequality Dynamics, Infrastructure Hub

**Key Research Angles:**
1. **Information/Communication**
   - Casa de Contratación vs. Casa da Índia comparison
   - Intelligence networks in Flanders, Italy, Americas
   - Geographic knowledge accumulation and protection
   - Publishing/printing capacity despite Inquisition

2. **Economic/Fiscal/Financial — 6 Sub-Components**
   
   a) **Central Banking/Monetary Authority**
   - NO central bank (Banco de San Carlos not until 1782)
   - Monetary policy via silver flows
   - Currency management and debasement
   
   b) **Commercial/Investment Banking**
   - Genoese asiento system — dependency vs. partnership?
   - Fugger relationship evolution
   - Medici and German banker relationships
   - Portuguese converso bankers post-1580
   - Grafe/Irigoin: Domestic credit networks underestimated?
   
   c) **Securities Markets/Exchange**
   - NO formal exchange (Madrid not until 1831)
   - Juro (government bond) market — sophistication levels
   - Secondary trading of juros
   
   d) **Insurance/Risk Management**
   - Bilbao and Burgos insurance practices
   - Maritime insurance development
   - Consulate court systems
   
   e) **Fiscal Capacity**
   - Millones, alcabala, Indies revenues
   - Álvarez-Nogal/Chamley: Bankruptcy as negotiation, not failure
   - Drelichman/Voth: Philip II's debt as sustainable
   - Serial bankruptcies (1557, 1575, 1596, 1607, 1627, 1647) — reinterpretation
   
   f) **Foreign Creditor Dependency**
   - Genoese share of financing — actual percentages
   - Shift from Fuggers to Genoese to Portuguese conversos
   - Comparison to Dutch/Venetian indigenous development

3. **The Resource Curse Question**
   - ~181,000 metric tons of American silver
   - Dutch Disease dynamics — evidence quality?
   - Hamilton's Price Revolution thesis and critiques
   - Why no indigenous financial innovation despite resources?

4. **Inequality Dynamics** (Cross-Cutting)
   - Wealth concentration patterns
   - Land distribution (grandes, hidalgos, Church)
   - Colonial extraction beneficiaries
   - Elite capture of reform attempts

5. **Infrastructure Hub** (Cross-Cutting)
   - Seville → Cadiz transition
   - Caribbean/Pacific network structure
   - Chokepoint control (Caribbean, Panama, Philippines)
   - Network density vs. Dutch/English competitors

**Key Scholars to Engage:**
- Álvarez-Nogal & Chamley (bankruptcy reinterpretation)
- Drelichman & Voth (Philip II debt sustainability)
- Grafe & Irigoin (fiscal federalism, North-Weingast critique)
- Hamilton (Price Revolution — with critiques)
- Flynn & Giráldez (silver global flows)
