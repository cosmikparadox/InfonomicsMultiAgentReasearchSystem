Key Findings

  1. Economic/Fiscal domain showed first stress in 1585 — the Great Debasement preceded military, governance, and information failures by decades, establishing the economic domain as the lead indicator of systemic decline.
  2. Textbook Minsky moment 1854–1875 — the Ottoman debt trajectory from zero external borrowing to sovereign default in 21 years maps precisely onto Minsky’s hedge → speculative → Ponzi finance framework.
  3. Information infrastructure collapse preceded political dissolution — the degradation of the ulak-menzilhane communication system and failure to adopt print technology created critical feedback-loop failures before territorial losses accelerated.

Minsky Moment Analysis

Hedge Finance (1299–1520)

Zero external borrowing. Fiscal deficits managed through the timar system and occasional currency debasement. Income from conquest covered current expenditure and principal obligations. The empire operated as a self-financing military-fiscal state.

Speculative Finance (1520–1854)

Revenue covered operating expenses but not the growing structural gap. Debasement accelerated as implicit taxation. Short-term borrowing from Galata sarrafs and esham life annuities (from 1775) bridged deficits. The empire could service interest but relied on rolling over obligations and debasing the currency to cover principal shortfalls.

Ponzi Finance (1854–1875)

Fifteen foreign loans in 21 years. New borrowing required to service existing debt. By 1874, Galata bankers refused to lend even at 25% — the textbook credit-collapse moment. Annual debt service consumed 50%+ of state revenue. Default declared October 1875. The Decree of Muharrem (1881) surrendered fiscal sovereignty to the OPDA.

Minsky Moment: 1874 — Galata bankers refused lending at 25%, marking total credit-market collapse. The preceding trajectory (zero debt → 100–150% debt/GDP in two decades) is among the most compressed sovereign Minsky cycles in economic history.
